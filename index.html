    ClarkCant Can.

    Chapter one

    Clark couldn't.

    Clark Cant was our first AI agent, named as a joke on Clark Kent. In the beginning he could barely do anything. Every step ran into a guardrail: a refusal, a confirmation, a permission he didn't have.

    Honestly? He was almost useless.

    Chapter two

    Then Clark could.

    The guardrails didn't vanish. They moved into the system underneath: work confined to the folders you allow, a visible trail of what he did, and a Stop button that always works. The wall of dialogs came down.

    Clark became a teammate, and helped us build more than one product.

    Chapter three

    ClarkCant Can.

    We kept the name as a reminder of where he started. ClarkCant is that teammate, opened up for everyone: one conversation, one voice, and an agent that gets on with what you asked.

    Talk, don't operate

    One conversation.
    Everything else is plumbing.

    Most agent tools hand you the machinery and wish you luck. ClarkCant keeps it where it belongs: underneath. You never need to learn how an agent works to get something done.

    What you see.

    • SessionsClark keeps one conversation. You never pick a session.
    • Context windowsWhat to remember and what to drop is his problem.
    • Models & routingChoose one if you care. Otherwise, don't.
    • Skills & toolsFound and used when the task needs them.
    • SubagentsIf work splits, it comes back into the same thread.
    • MCP serversConnected through chat, not config files.
    • Hooks & workflowsBehaviour you describe, not wiring you draw.
    • Runtimes & nodesYour laptop, your server. Same conversation.
    • PoliciesPick how bold Clark is, once. Change it by asking.
    • Audit trailEvery action written down, there when you look.

    What Clark handles.

    Rich replies

    Sometimes the best answer
    isn't text.

    When a list, a chart or a tiny app says it better, Clark draws one right inside the reply. No new window, no new app to learn. Go on, use these.

    Examples built for this page to show the idea, with made-up data. In ClarkCant, widgets and mini-apps render inside the conversation, and every tool Clark uses appears as a widget you can open to see exactly what it did.

    Voice

    Voice is the
    same Clark.

    Not a separate assistant with a separate memory. Speak and it lands in the same conversation, with the same context and the same abilities. Start typing, finish talking, or the other way round.

    1. Draft a reply to Linh about moving Friday's meeting
    2. Drafted. Want me to suggest Monday at 10?
    3. “Make it Tuesday, and a bit warmer.”
    4. Updated. Tuesday, and friendlier.

    Uses your microphone only to move the Orb. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

    What we hold ourselves to

    AI should make computers
    easier, not more complicated.

    1. Conversation is the app.

      No dashboard to learn, no sidebar of modes. Asking, seeing results, changing settings: it all happens in one place.

    2. Talk, don't operate.

      Say the outcome you want. Finding the right tab, tool or command is Clark's job.

    3. Voice is the same Clark.

      One agent, one memory, whether you type or speak.

    4. Sometimes the best answer isn't text.

      Tables, charts and small apps appear right in the reply, when they help.

    5. Power underneath. Not in your face.

      Details stay out of the way until you ask for them or something needs you.

    6. Don't make people learn the architecture.

      Nobody should need to understand sessions, runtimes or servers to finish a task.

    7. Honest, always.

      No fake progress bars, no “done” that isn't. If Clark doesn't know, he says so.

    Early days v0.2 · bootstrap, not a release

    We'd rather tell you where things stand than impress you. The foundations are in and tested; parts of the experience are still being built.

    Working today

    • A conversation that streams replies and draws tool calls as widgets
    • A headless runtime you can run on your own machine or server
    • Read-only search across your files
    • Type / to pick a skill and @ to point at a project, a file or folder inside it, a service, another conversation or background work. What you picked shows as a chip, deleting it from the text takes it back out, and your machine checks it again when you send: if it has gone, Clark says which one and keeps what you wrote. Pointing at something never gives Clark more access than it already had
    • An inbox you can act on: each notification offers what fits it, like opening its conversation or asking Clark what it means and what to do next, typed or spoken. You can also put a notification on the message you are writing, mark it unread again, or undo dismissing it for five minutes. You can snooze one until later (in an hour, this evening, tomorrow morning or next Monday) and it comes back unread when the time is up, or stop Clark notifying you about one kind of thing, such as one automation or one repository, and turn it back on from the inbox. Reminders and anything waiting on your decision are never silenced. A notification also leads with what you can do about it: run background work again when it failed or was stopped, ask a question again when it expired with nobody answering, update a package to the version it names, or skip that version, which you can take back later from the inbox. When one of those can't be done any more, it says why instead of offering a button that fails
    • The Orb in your own style: pick one of six looks, or tune your own within safe limits, in Settings or just by asking Clark. It changes right away and stays after a reload, and Clark only says it changed once the Orb shows it. If your system asks for reduced motion, that still wins
    • Stop, and an audit trail of every action
    • Open interfaces for your own apps and AI tools: a REST API with streaming, MCP, WebSocket and a CLI, all on one token (docs)
    • Packages that bring their own service, which Clark can call, each in a locked-down container with no network (needs Docker or Podman)
    • Buttons Clark places in the conversation that do what they say: pin a view, ask Clark for something, or call a package's service. A button that cannot run right now is shown disabled, with the reason
    • Views Clark lays out for what you asked, from the widgets your machine has: a grid, cards, tabs and sections that open. They fit a phone screen, and reopen the same from your history
    • Forms and lists Clark places for you to fill in or pick from, and a search box that narrows the table beside it. Your machine checks what you send before anything runs, and a form never asks for a password
    • Cards that show where something stands, in Clark's own words: a status with its tone spelled out, progress as a bar or as steps with the current one marked, and a few labelled facts. A status or progress card says when its information was from, or that it is what Clark stated and at what time, and no card claims to be live. Your machine refuses one that doesn't add up, such as a count above its total, or text with a hidden character that would make it read differently from how it looks
    • Parts of a view that work together: pick what a chart shows, or search a table, and the rest of the view follows. Your machine keeps what you picked, so the view looks the same when you come back
    • Clark knows what you changed on a view: after you pick a filter or search a table, your next question is answered with that in mind, and speaking works the same way. Only what changed is passed along, trimmed and cleaned first
    • Code, changes and files Clark shows you as it works: a block of code with its line numbers and a copy button, a diff with each added and removed line marked and counted, and a card naming a file it made. They show only what Clark wrote, as text, and open or fetch nothing. A hidden character that could make code read differently from how it looks is shown as a visible marker, with a warning on the card, and your machine refuses a diff whose numbers don't add up
    • Work Clark does in the background stays inside what it was given: only the folders named for it, and a repository is changed in Clark's own copy, never in the files you have open. Its commands follow the same rules, audit and Stop as the ones you see in the conversation, and anything that would need your approval is left undone and said so
    • Things you ask for once and Clark does on its own later: say "when this happens, do that", "every 30 minutes" or "at 9 tomorrow", and Clark reminds you or starts the work when it does, in the same conversation and in your inbox. Anything outside the conversation can tell Clark something happened, it never runs twice for the same event, and you can list, pause, change or remove what you set up just by asking
    • GitHub can be one of those things: ask Clark to act when an issue is labelled, a pull request opens or a check fails, through your repository's webhook wherever GitHub can reach your machine. Where it can't, such as a laptop behind a home router, there is nothing to set up: your machine checks the repository named in what you asked for every few minutes instead, and a private repository is read with the GitHub token you give Clark. If GitHub keeps refusing, you're told once in your inbox, with what to do. Every delivery is checked against a secret only your machine holds, what your own Clark did on GitHub doesn't set it off again, and work only starts in a copy of that same repository
    • That work can go all the way to a draft pull request: Clark fixes the issue on its own branch, in a separate copy that leaves your checkout exactly as it was, runs your tests, pushes and opens the pull request, then tells you in the conversation with a link to it. If a test fails, it tells you the work isn't done, and which step failed. If a push is cut off before GitHub answers, because it ran out of time, you stopped it or your machine restarted, Clark doesn't push again on its own: it tells you once in your inbox that it can't tell whether the push went through, so you can check GitHub first. Then one press on that notice says whether it did, and the task ends the way it really went. That answer is yours to give: Clark can't give it for itself, and once given it can't be changed. The GitHub token you give it only reaches git, gh and the checks on your repository described above, and it is kept out of the conversation and out of what those commands print
    • Any service that can sign its requests with a shared secret can start things too: name it in the conversation, give Clark its secret through the secure form, and only deliveries signed with that secret count. Clarks you have paired work the same way with each other: news from one can start what you set up on the other, and a task you set up on one can run on the other, but only in the folders and with the permissions that Clark's owner allowed. Anything beyond that waits for them, and you're told while it waits, since only they can decide; if they say no, or never answer, your task ends there. A time limit you set for each run holds on the other Clark too, pausing or removing what you set up takes back the permission it had there, stopping it where you set it up stops it on the other Clark too, and both conversations hear how it went. Before you set one up, Clark can tell you what the other Clark can run for you right now, and warns you when it isn't allowed or isn't ready yet. A task that arrives before the other Clark is ready waits there until it is, and both of you are told what it is waiting for. Files that run writes can come back to you with the result, once both of you have allowed a size for them. If one message between them is lost, the ones after it still arrive, and both of you are told what was lost and what it means for your tasks. Tested between two Clarks on the same machine; pairing across two separate machines is still being verified

    Try it from source

    Needs Node.js 22.19+. The installer clones the repo, then walks you through the model, your key and where Clark runs: on this machine or in Docker.

    # macOS / Linux
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/digitopvn/clarkcant/main/tools/install.sh | sh
    # Windows PowerShell
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/digitopvn/clarkcant/main/tools/install.ps1 | iex
